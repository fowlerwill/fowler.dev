title: The Jetsons might have had it right
description: but what does it take for George to push the button?
date: 2026-08-10

George woke up. He felt it before his eyes opened. The grit of his tongue stuck
to the roof of his mouth, the feeling of that layer of grime on his teeth, he'd
been drinking again last night. Now comes the headache. Still with his eyes closed
he braces for that familiar constant bitter hum of disapproval from his brain,
starved for water. The eyes open now, harsh bright light is too much shock for
his body to take just yet, he closes them again, and rolls over - he knows he
can get away with sleeping in another half an hour.

He knows it's trite, but the thought of work makes him tremble. Reporting into
work, sitting in front of that giant screen, he doesn't have any friends at
work, everyone prefers to work from home, they don't want to make the effort to
befriend anyone. The loneliness at times makes him feel like crying, but then
he remembers that his role is to be the strong one, to feed his family and
not need friends, not need any acquaintance other than his wife and children,
they should be wholly fulfilling. At least that's what Dude Dad on TikTok says.

Spacely makes sprockets, they need to make sprockets at an astounding rate.
Early on in the company, the bottleneck was manufacturing. Now, abundance has
dissolved the moat of the skilled worker. Securityvisors watched the machines,
and occasionally helped when they got stuck, or when luddites attacked, but
now the bottleneck was in the hearts and minds of the populace - they
truly didn't know how many sprockets they needed, and the role of Spacely's
was to invent ways to inform them how many sprockets they needed (and just
a hint, it was directly correlated to the balance the bank would lend them)

So Spacely devised a new program. This system would invent new companies at
a rate of thousands per day, researching minute-by-minute flash trends
on social media and attaching the value of sprockets to social wants through
astroturfing via millions of AI accounts. It would simultaneously lobby local,
provincial, and federal governments for amenable laws to the production of
sprockets out of the material du jour (or minute, as the trend may dictate).
It would research and phish executives at friendly companies to procure
large deals for sprockets at bulk rates, all valued on a surge pricing model
that ensured maximal value to Spacely. And it would start rumour mills
(again via the astroturfing accounts) about competitor sprockets, diminishing
their value, exposing bad behaviour by executives, and doxing them when
necessary to redirect popular discontent against the sprocket industry.

George's terminal was fabulous, it was the best in ergonomics that money could
buy. The screen was an immaculate representation of pixels, blending reality
with the digital information in front of him. His chair had the best neck support,
his benefit program had regular massages where he could relieve the stress that
built up in him from the terror of his work, up to five times per year. It was
an enviable position from his peers' vantage. The money came in, he could support
his wife and daughter's spending habits, he had time off to play ball with his
son, and even time to walk the dog.

Work though... work made him sweat cold. They had long given up on him examining
the details of individual companies in their approval to operate. His conscience
was a bottleneck they couldn't afford. So he now reviewed them in batches
of hundreds at a time. His job was to push a button. His job was to understand
what all of those shell companies would do with their directives, understand it,
extrapolate where they might go with it, and push the button to accept responsibility
for their actions.

George pushed the button.

He went home and drank.
